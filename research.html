What We Do

Research

Soils are among the most complex systems on Earth, with tightly interconnected processes governing carbon storage, microbial activity, and water movement. The Soil Systems Lab bridges structural and biogeochemical approaches across multiple scales to advance our understanding of soil as a living system.

Research Area 01

Soil Pore Structure and Carbon Dynamics

Soils hold enormous potential as carbon stores. We examine how pore structure drives carbon sequestration and greenhouse gas emissions, aiming to understand why organic matter persists over varying timescales. By linking physical architecture to biogeochemical function, we work to uncover the structural controls on soil carbon stability.

Research Area 02

Methodological Development — Imaging & Experimental Innovation

Understanding soil requires tools that preserve its integrity during analysis. We develop high-resolution, spatially resolved techniques using both laboratory and synchrotron-based approaches. These methods allow us to study soil structure, chemistry, and biology while maintaining the soil's natural architecture, opening new windows into processes that were previously invisible.

Research Area 03

Agricultural and Environmental Applications

Human management and a changing climate are reshaping soils worldwide. We investigate how tillage, irrigation, crop rotations, and shifting climate conditions alter soil structure and function at multiple scales. Our goal is to translate fundamental insights into practical guidance for sustainable land management and climate resilience.
