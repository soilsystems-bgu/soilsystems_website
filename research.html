Research

Soils are among the most complex systems on Earth. Carbon storage, microbial activity, root–microbiome interactions, and water movement are deeply interconnected, and much of this functioning is governed by soil structure: the physical arrangement of pores, particles, and interfaces.

How pore-scale architecture connects to system-level behavior remains one of the more challenging and unresolved questions in soil science. Our overarching goal is bridging structural and biogeochemical approaches to understand soils as integrated systems across scales, from individual pores to agricultural fields.

Soil-on-a-chip: how pore networks govern carbon dynamics

How do soil pore networks regulate microbial activity and carbon cycling? This project combines microfluidics, X-ray tomography, and image analysis to study these processes in controlled, soil-like pore structures.
